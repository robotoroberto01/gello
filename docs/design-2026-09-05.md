# GELLO leader arm: design brief, 5 September 2026

A half-scale copy of the RobotDoctor arm's kinematics with a cheap DYNAMIXEL in every joint and a trigger in the
grip. You move the small arm; the big arm follows, joint for joint. It is the teleoperation and demonstration
device of the ALOHA / GELLO papers (Wu et al. 2023, "GELLO: a general, low-cost and intuitive teleoperation
framework"), built the way that repo builds them, on our own arm's geometry.

Tyler's decision, 09-05: build it alongside the wheel-legged balancer (its own repo).

## Why a leader arm, and why this one

- **Hand-guiding was decided for the arm on 09-05** (the dead-man in the probe hand's handle). A leader arm is the
  other half of that: guiding at a distance, with the operator's hand off the patient, and every motion recorded
  as joint trajectories that a policy can later learn from.
- **Joint-for-joint mapping needs no IK.** The leader has the arm's link ratios (320:400 upper:fore, J2 65 mm up
  from J1, wrist offset 27.5), so its joint angles ARE the arm's targets. The arm's existing runner takes joint
  targets and owns the PD, gravity term, joint limits and the supervisor. `gello/follow.py` is a rate limiter and
  a serial port.
- **The servos are encoders here.** Torque stays off; the XL330's 12-bit absolute encoder is what we pay for. A
  soft "hold" (current mode, 60 mA) parks the arm when the 't' key is sent.
- **Half scale** because the arm's reach (720 mm J2 to wrist) at full size is a desk-filling leader; at 0.5 the
  handle traces the tool's path at half size in a 360 mm sphere, which is the ergonomic range of a seated hand.

## Geometry (`cad/params.py`, `cad/gello.py`)

| | arm | leader (x 0.5) |
|---|---|---|
| J1 output face -> J2 axis | 130 | 65 |
| J2 -> J3 | 320 | 160 |
| J3 -> wrist centre (J5) | 400 | 200 |
| J4 horn -> J5 axis | 55 | 27.5 |
| grip along the tool axis | probe | 90 mm cylinder, d28, trigger under the index finger |
| joint conventions | J1 yaw Z, J2/J3 pitch X (J2 from hanging), J4 roll, J5 pitch, J6 roll | the same |

Every joint is one servo. Its case is carried by the upstream link in a printed cradle (two M2 screws a side
through the case's holes); its horn drives the downstream link. Pitch joints (J2, J3, J5) get a yoke on the driven
link: an ear on the horn (4 x M2 into the horn) and an ear on the idler bearing, so the joint is held on both sides
and the horn carries no bending. J4 is a plate on the horn. J6 is mounted inverted: its case sits in the grip and
its horn points up into a plate under the tool link's yoke, because a servo's case is behind its horn and a
horn-down J6 would put that case inside the wrist. The trigger servo sits in the grip with a 40 mm lever the finger
pulls; a rubber band returns it.

The yoke's crossbar sweeps a 29 mm circle round each pitch axis, so nothing of the carrying link may stand inside
it: the shoulder's post rises behind the J2 case, the J5 case points up the wrist, the wrist's post starts above
the sweep. `cad/gello.py --limits` sweeps each pitch joint until the links meet: J2 travels from 20 deg (the yoke
meets the base) to past the arm's 178 stop, J3 folds +/-110 from straight, J5 +/-110. Those are the leader's
ranges; `calibration.json` clamps them to the arm's.

Printed: base, shoulder, upper arm, forearm, wrist, tool, handle, trigger lever: ~145 g of PETG at 40 %. Seven
servos: 126 g. The whole leader is ~300 g.

**Desk mounting.** The arm's home pose (J2 110, J3 -70, J5 20) points the tool down at the exam table, 130 mm
below J2. The leader's J2 is 65 mm up, so the grip lands 36 mm BELOW the desk in that pose. Clamp the base at the
desk's edge with the arm over the edge, or put it on a 150 mm riser. The rubber-band post is on the base for the
counterbalance (a band from the post to the upper arm's yoke holds the arm up when the hand lets go; GELLO uses
the same trick).

Bus wiring is one twisted trio (5 V, GND, DATA) down the links with a plug at each servo. Torque off, so the bus
carries a few hundred milliamps at most; 26 AWG is fine.

## Software

- `firmware/gello_node/gello_node.ino`: Protocol 2.0 reads of Present Position (addr 132) on IDs 1-7 at 100 Hz,
  streamed as one ASCII line a sample. 'z' marks a zero pose, 't' toggles the parking hold.
- `gello/protocol.py`: the line parser and `Calibration` (zero counts, signs, offsets, limits; unwraps to +/- 180).
- `gello/reader.py`: serial reader, `--calibrate` captures the zero pose.
- `gello/follow.py`: leader angles -> rate-limited joint targets for RobotDoctor's runner (`--sim` drives the
  arm's MuJoCo model through `robot/control/kinematics.Arm`); the trigger becomes the gripper's close fraction.

The sign of each joint is found once: move J1 clockwise from above, the arm's J1 target must go the same way; flip
`sign[i]` in `calibration.json` where it does not. Limits in the calibration clamp the leader to the arm's ranges
so a leader flung past a stop cannot ask the arm for an angle its stops forbid.

## Sequence

1. Print the set in PLA (2 hours), fit-check the cradle on an XC330 when they arrive (same case), adjust `FIT`
   and `side_holes` in `cad/servos.py` to the calipers.
2. Order 7 XL330s and cables, one more ESP32-S3.
3. Flash the node, `uv run python -m gello.reader` on raw counts; assign IDs 1-7 with the ROBOTIS Wizard first.
4. `--calibrate`, set signs, `uv run python -m gello.follow --sim`: the simulated arm follows the leader.
5. The arm itself, when it exists, through its supervisor.

## Open

- XL330 case dimensions and hole positions are from memory of the e-manual drawing (VERIFY every number in
  `cad/servos.py` on the first servo; the CAD is parametric on them).
- The J5 range on the leader is +/-100; the arm's is the hand's band (`H.J5_RANGE`). The calibration's limits
  should be regenerated from the arm's `sim/arm.xml` ranges rather than typed.
- Gravity compensation on the leader (GELLO's optional mode: torque on, current = a gravity model) would let go of
  the rubber band; the XL330's 0.52 N.m is ample for a 300 g arm. Later.
